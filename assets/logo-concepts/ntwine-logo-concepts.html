Ntwine — icon concepts

Fifteen directions for the mark after the rename, each drawn as real geometry and rendered at 512, 128, 32 and 16 px. Every concept is judged at 16px, not at hero size — the favicon is the constraint, so the small render is the evidence and the big one is the advert.

The 16px rule

A 512-unit canvas maps to 16 px, so one pixel is 32 units. That single conversion decides what is drawable and rules out most of what a logo wants to be.

≥ 52uminimum stroke. Below this the mark dissolves into a smudge.
≥ 64uminimum ink gap or counter. Below this two features fuse, or a hole fills.
3elements across the width. That is the whole budget — 288 usable units buys no more.

Why the current mark fails

The fraying rope reads beautifully large and is structurally impossible small. Its five loose fibres are drawn at stroke-width 16–19 on a 512 canvas — half a pixel at favicon size. They do not thin out; they cease to exist, and what survives is an amber smear.

Concepts

Converge shortlist

Many agents come back to one queue

The truer half of Ntwine. Work fans out, but the whole point of the app is that it all returns to one place you read top to bottom. Three strands run parallel, then merge low into a single root.

At 16px. Holds, and better than the fan — three parallel bars merging is a stable silhouette.

Risk. Splaying the arms straight makes it an aeroplane; the merge has to stay low and the bend has to stay soft.

Verdict. The one idea about the product rather than the word. Stable silhouette, no glyph collision.

Curl shortlist

Ntwine, literally — one strand curling

A closed loop and one tail sweeping the other way — a tendril, not a numeral.

At 16px. Holds. The counter is 132 units across, so the hole stays open — though at exactly 16px it reads as a notch rather than a clean round hole.

Risk. Drawn with the tail on the loop's left it is unmistakably a 6, and no amount of tilt fixes that. The tail has to leave from the right and sweep back across, which is the whole design.

Verdict. Survives smallest, says the name, and is the only mark there that is both distinctive and structurally safe.

Loose end shortlist

A single strand, curling free

Maximum legibility: one element, a 74 stroke, a 74-unit counter under the hook, tilted so the return leg falls at an angle.

At 16px. The most robust mark here by a distance — one element, nothing that can fuse, one counter and it is 74 units wide.

Risk. Upright it is a candy cane. Even tilted it is quiet — there is very little idea in it.

Verdict. The safety option. Nothing will ever break it; nothing about it is memorable either.

Fan in play

One prompt becomes many agents

The product's core motion read bottom-up — a single trunk splitting into three that sweep out and away.

At 16px. Holds. Tips sit 136 units apart on a 54 stroke, so the gaps stay above 2px.

Risk. The most expected shape in the set. A Y-with-branches is close to a rune and to a dozen other dev-tool marks.

Verdict. Reads clearly; reads like everyone else's logo too.

Spark in play

Ntwine as static — everything going at once

Six bowed fibres from a common origin. Bowed, not straight, so it reads as hair with a charge in it rather than a sparkle or an asterisk.

At 16px. Holds. Radial symmetry is the friendliest thing you can hand a 16px box — there is no wrong orientation and no dead corner.

Risk. Busiest mark here. Too much bow and it turns into an octopus.

Verdict. Most personality, most noise. Radial symmetry is a real asset for an icon.

Monogram in play

F, drawn in fibre

A wordmark-adjacent option: the arms end in curls rather than square cuts, so the letter carries the texture of the name.

At 16px. Holds as an F, though it drifts toward a P at the smallest size.

Risk. Safest identity play, least idea in it — and a letterform ties the mark to the name, so it cannot outlive a second rename.

Verdict. Safe, and welds the identity to the current name.

Plume in play

One root, swept

The fan with weather in it. Asymmetry gives the mark a direction and some personality the symmetric options do not have.

At 16px. Holds. The sweep survives because all three strands share one side.

Risk. Reads botanical — a wheat sheaf or a sprout — which is a whole other brand.

Verdict. Charming, but botanical — a different brand's mark.

Serpentine in play

Many threads, one continuous line

The queue drawn honestly: not a stack of separate cards but one thread laid back and forth, because that is what working a queue top to bottom actually is. Same silhouette as Stack, with a reason behind it.

At 16px. Holds at 1.75px of gap, same as Stack — and unlike Stack the connected ends stop it reading as a menu icon.

Risk. The idea only lands at hero size. Small, it is a stack of bars with the ends joined, and nobody reads the join.

Verdict. Best idea-per-pixel of the queue concepts, but the idea is invisible small.

Tuft in play

One thread, two curls

A stem that splits into two outward curls. Outward curls are the single most unmistakable signal of ntwine, and two of them make a face-like, friendly mark.

At 16px. Holds if the curl radius stays at 68 or above; below that the counters seal shut.

Risk. Symmetrical curls can tip into horns or a moustache depending on the weight.

Verdict. Distinctive and friendly, but it is the Aries glyph and you will not un-see that.

Flick in play

A single strand with weight in it

The only tapered mark here — drawn as a filled shape, thick at the root and thinning as it curls. Tapering is what separates a drawn logo from a stroked one, and it is where the craft shows.

At 16px. Survives because the taper stops early: the tip stays 44 units wide, which is 1.4px, rather than running to a point.

Risk. A taper is the one thing that cannot be authored as a uniform stroke, so this mark is the most expensive to maintain and to redraw at other weights.

Verdict. The only drawn-not-stroked mark. Elegant, and the most work to maintain.

Twist weak

Parallel work, one cord

Two strands running in parallel and staying wound together — a real over-under weave, not two mirrored waves.

At 16px. Marginal. The casings close up as intended, but what is left is a knobbly column closer to a dollar sign than a cord.

Risk. Antiphase strands of equal weight make lens shapes that read as a stack of 8s; the alternating weave is what stops that.

Verdict. Degrades to a knobbly column.

Lanes weak

Three threads running; one needs you

The queue insight as a picture — most threads run clean, one has gone wild and wants your attention. The only concept that encodes the app's job rather than its subject matter.

At 16px. Holds, but the story does not: at 16px the curl is a bump on a bar and the mark reads as three tallies.

Risk. Needs the curl to be extreme to survive, and extreme unbalances the mark at hero size.

Verdict. The story disappears at 16px; only three tallies remain.

Stack weak

The queue itself

Honest about what you actually look at all day — a stack of cards receding upward. Widths taper so it reads as depth, not as a menu.

At 16px. Very legible and completely generic. Three rows leave only 1.9px of gap, so it is also the concept the 16px rule punishes hardest.

Risk. One notch away from a hamburger icon, and it depicts the container rather than the brand.

Verdict. A hamburger menu with extra steps.

Knot ruled out

Two strands, tied

A single overhand loop with two tails crossing below it. The one mark here with genuine craft in it at hero size.

At 16px. Fails. The crossings merge and the two tails close up into a capital A. Measured, not predicted — the 16px render is below.

Risk. Meaning fights the product: a knot says tangled and stuck, which is the state Ntwine exists to prevent.

Verdict. Collapses to a capital A, and its meaning is the opposite of the product's.

Coil ruled out

Ntwine as a texture, not an object

A curly-cord corkscrew — two real loops, not a wave. The most literal picture of the word, and the least literal about the product.

At 16px. Fails, and it is the only outright failure in the set. Two loops inside 288 units leaves ~30 units of counter — under 1px — so at 16px the holes fill and it becomes a lump. Shown unfudged.

Risk. The arithmetic kills it: any coil with more than one turn cannot clear the 2px counter floor at favicon size. This direction is off the table unless the favicon is allowed to be a different, simpler mark than the logo.

Verdict. Cannot clear the counter floor at any pitch above one turn. Arithmetically excluded.

How to read the verdicts. They are a reading of the 16px renders on this page, not something the geometry decides. Two marks are ruled out by measurement rather than taste: Coil cannot clear the counter floor at any pitch above one turn, and Knot collapses to a capital A while also meaning the opposite of what the product does.

What is not decided here. Colour is carried over from the existing mark (#e8b923 on #0d0e10) so the concepts compare on form alone. The dark rounded tile guarantees favicon contrast against any browser theme, but every mark would also work knocked out on a flat field.
